# Legacy — superseded work, kept for the record

Nothing in this directory is current. It is preserved because it is the
historical record of the thesis work, and because a retracted number is more
useful when you can see what it was and why it changed than when it silently
disappears.

**Do not cite any figure from this directory.** Current status is in
[`../demo/results/CROSS_DATASET_FINDINGS.md`](../demo/results/CROSS_DATASET_FINDINGS.md),
[`../demo/results/BENCHMARK.md`](../demo/results/BENCHMARK.md), and
[`../README.md`](../README.md).

## `resubstitution-results/`

The 2026-08-20 eleven-dataset outputs. Their off-diagonal cells were genuine
cross-dataset tests, but the diagonal trained and evaluated on the same rows.
The published 0.995 in-domain AUC and 0.487 gap are therefore resubstitution
artifacts. The 2026-08-22 audit also changed row retention, Bot-IoT sampling,
IoT-23 cache invalidation and small-budget threshold handling, so all exact
tables are quarantined together until a full rerun is possible.
The directory's `models/` contains the associated 12-feature binary ONNX and
metadata. They are research artifacts and are not compatible with the live
22-feature daemon.

## `pre-remediation-results/`

Every artifact here was generated on 2026-08-03 by the SFAF pipeline **as it
stood before the 2026-08-19 review**, and is invalidated by at least one of the
critical findings fixed since:

| finding | what it invalidates |
|---|---|
| F01 — feature mappings semantically wrong | any figure derived from the aligned feature space: port numbers were mapped into packet-length slots, byte counts into rate slots, and flow duration was left in microseconds for some datasets and seconds for others |
| F02 — cross-domain F1 includes degenerate classifiers | any "generalization" claim scored with F1 alone, at a fixed 0.5 threshold, on a ~50/50 test set where predicting "attack" for everything scores 0.667 |
| F03 — `xgb_edge.onnx` exported with the wrong scaler | the edge-deployment latency/size tables, which measured a model fed mis-normalised input |

`report_numbers.md` is the file to be most careful with: it is titled *"Key
Numbers for Report"* and contains instructions of the form *"REPLACE table
with…"*. Its headline claims — SFAF unified at 92.68% on UNSW-NB15, a
**+56.59 pp** generalisation gain, cross-dataset transfer as a solved problem —
are exactly what the corrected study contradicts. The eleven-dataset rerun puts
cross-domain transfer at **ROC-AUC 0.509 against a chance baseline of 0.500**,
with MCC −0.007. The gain was, in large part, a measurement of the
misalignment.

`results/` holds the earlier RandomForest/XGBoost single-dataset reports and
plots from the same era. They are not wrong in the same way — they were never
cross-dataset claims — but they were generated by code paths that no longer
exist and nothing in the repo references them.

## `notebooks/`

The three original Jupyter notebooks. They are the readable narrative of how
the project was built, and they were the source of the numbers above.

`02_SFAF_Unified_Model.ipynb` still contains the retracted mapping literally:

```python
'src_port':'Min Packet Length','dst_port':'Max Packet Length',
```

They are kept unmodified rather than patched — a corrected notebook that was
never re-run would be a third artifact claiming to be authoritative. The
maintained, runnable equivalents are `code/multidataset.py` (alignment),
`code/02_train_sfaf.py` (training), and `code/cross_dataset_eval.py` (the
study). Those are linted and covered by the test suite; these are not, and are
excluded from both.
